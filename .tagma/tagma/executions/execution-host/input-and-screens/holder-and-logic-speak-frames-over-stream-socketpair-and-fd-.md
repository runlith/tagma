The holder (`native/holder/src/frames.rs`) and the logic client (`src/holder-client/frames.ts`) read the same frames and change only together: holder and logic are always from one build, there is no second frame shape. A frame knows no OS: a signal travels by name, a refusal by a neutral cause, birth by a platform-units string, capabilities declared by HELLO.

- **Frame handles are those arriving with it plus those named in it.** On POSIX copies travel via SCM_RIGHTS and the list in the frame is empty; a platform duplicating handles into the logic process before the frame writes their values into the list. The client joins both and checks against the count the record names, so neither side branches on OS.
- **fds arrive no later than their frame.** Copies travel via SCM_RIGHTS on the frame's first `sendmsg`, and the kernel delivers them with the first bytes of that `sendmsg`. So the client reads to one frame's boundary and binds the arrived fds to it, as many as the frame names. A frame carries at most a few fds: one `recvmsg`'s control buffer must not truncate them — the kernel silently closes truncated ones, and the record would be left without its fds.
- **A queued frame keeps its record's fds open.** `release` or input end removes fds from the record at once, but a frame carrying them may still wait for socket room; a closed number would hand already-foreign fds to logic, and a copy per frame would double the holder's fd spend exactly when new logic is not reading the channel yet. So the record and the frames share one fd, closed when both let go of it.
- **Exit observed without reaping, on SIGCHLD and logic request.** An exit already found is announced with an `exited` frame before answering `observe` or `reap`, so logic does not guess it from frame-delivery delay. Release allowed after reap; children of a rejected launch the holder kills and reaps with a non-blocking SIGCHLD pass, never handing live ownership to orphan. SIGCHLD kept at `SIG_DFL`: with `SIG_IGN` the kernel would reap children itself and statuses lost. `waitid` on macOS also answers for a stopped child (`CLD_STOPPED`) even when asked only `WEXITED` (probe on macOS 26), so only `CLD_EXITED`, `CLD_KILLED` and `CLD_DUMPED` count as exit: otherwise SIGSTOP or the leader's Ctrl-Z would close its input as after exit.
- **Group equals pid.** The holder launches each child with `POSIX_SPAWN_SETSID`, and while the leader is unreaped no other process can take its pid and id group; so a signal to the group allowed until reaped, refused after. macOS answers EPERM on `kill(-pgid)` to a group with one remaining exited unreaped leader; the holder, having already observed the exit and found no other members in the group, answers `gone` — the work is over — while EPERM with a live leader stays `denied`.
- **The holder leaves on its own only on `vacate`.** After logic that said `vacate` leaves, it kills the group and the leader of each record it did not release, reaps them and exits ([[1d5d6619]]). Logic that left without `vacate` restarts — at once if it reached ready or lived long, with a growing pause on falls before ready; logic that served and was killed is not punished with a loader-fall pause; and on ACTIVATE permission at once from the next bundle ([[d128a62a]]). The holder gets the logic program in argv and relaunches the same until it swaps bundles; after the swap by the argv the accepted PREPARE named. The next logic starts only after the former is reaped: two logics never live at once on the same records and one database. Logic that closed the channel and did not exit within the allotted term the holder finishes itself, otherwise the hung former would leave the records without logic forever.
- **A torn channel with live processes is a logic failure.** If the holder cannot read or write the channel or gets a frame that never happens, it finalizes logic, and the next starts with a clean channel and a full table. Logic that gets an unreadable frame from the holder answers `lost` with cause `broken` and closes its end; channel EOF is `gone`, i.e. the holder's death. With a torn channel logic does not wait for the holder to finalize it but leaves at once, as on yielding: a candidate that passed its checks it permits with the `ACTIVATE_EXIT` exit status (75) instead of an ACTIVATE frame, and the holder takes `bundle.next` by its `holder.entry`; without a candidate it exits 0, and the holder raises the standing logic on a clean channel. Otherwise a fix delivered by the candidate could never stand: prepare and yielding would go through the same torn channel.
