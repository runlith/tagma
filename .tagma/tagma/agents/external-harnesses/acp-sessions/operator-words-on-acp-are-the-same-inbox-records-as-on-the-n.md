The app shows pending words and buttons from the snapshot's `pendingInput`, and the snapshot derives them from the inbox by kernel rules. A steer is a reader `signal` in `queued` state, a follow-up is a `turn` in `queued` state, and `delivered` means the turn has already read the word. So the thread on `acp` records the same records with the same constructors (`inboxSteerValues`, `inboxTurnValues`). There is no separate record or snapshot for ACP: otherwise the thread on `acp` would have no badge, Tab would be the same steer, and cancel would pass the agent by.

- **Which record.** The kernel scheduler decides, not the driver: it records the word and in the same act hands it to the driver (`TransportRuns.deliver`, transport `words: 'receipt'`), so a turn that ended or changed in the meantime does not receive someone else's word: a `steer` with another turn's `expectedRunId` keeps the word as a follow-up or a new turn and answers where it stands. Enter on a turn that reads words mid-turn (the `steer: step` capability: the profile declares it, the agent confirms `_tagma/steer`, a prompt goes, not a compaction) records a steer. Tab, and Enter on a turn that does not read words (Devin, compaction), records a follow-up.
- **A steer stays `queued` until the echo.** The driver hands the agent each readable record (`signal`, `envelope`) as a separate `_tagma/steer` with its id — a row waiting for the next turn ([[fa4f58b1]]) only together with words the step requires — and puts an input claim on it without changing state (`claimInput`). No words go into a turn being cancelled. The claim excludes the record from `launchItems` so it does not fire twice, but in the snapshot it stays an unread steer until the echo. A word counts as handed only with the host `Admitted` receipt on the `_tagma/steer` frame: that receipt's step carries the `handed` report to the kernel, which publishes the thread change after commit, and only such a word, still unread by the agent, does the transport call handed (`TransportRuns.handed`). A hosted-call wait yields only to it, and the call reply names all such words ([[8e0f35a1|hosting]]). A claim with no receipt does not count as handed: a frame lost before recording (server restart, host refusal) the agent never receives, and the runner waiting on it would hold the call result until the turn end. If handing immediately made the record `delivered`, the badge would vanish in the same millisecond, and the word would appear in the feed only a step later — minutes into a long tool call.
- **Fate by agent reply.** The echo is `confirmInput`, and the read word is recorded with a native `signal` event by `RunReports.fate('read')` itself (`signalEntryId` of the record) into the turn that read it. An unconfirmed word is recorded by `fate('unconfirmed')` into the turn it was given to, with the same record marked `unconfirmed` and with the model message: the agent may have read it, so the model history holds it, and the feed shows it marked rather than losing it. A record already standing in the journal — the task that opened the turn, or the letter that woke the thread — gets the mark in a new revision with no second model message. A later echo only removes the mark in place: same turn, same position, the model message already stands. Unconfirmed words do not enter the turn end's processed requests. While the turn prompt is still open, `promptRequired` keeps the record under claim until the turn end: otherwise the driver would hand it again to a turn that does not take it. The turn end removes the claim from already-answered steers and compaction requests with the same record and reports them fate `returned`: otherwise a removed claim would stay in `WordsInFlight` and hold up the following words. `promptRequired` after the turn end, an unsent request, and a -32800 refusal with no pending `_tagma/withdraw` return the record to the queue. A -32800 refusal with a pending withdraw is a withdraw. A refusal where the agent certainly did not read the word (-32002: the process holds no session; -32601: no such method) returns the record just like `promptRequired`. Any other refusal, and a steer that became undetermined (a lost record receipt, a restart before frame recording), make the record `delivered`, unconfirmed: the agent may have read it. Steering of an undetermined steer stays, and a later echo still confirms it.
- **Preparation outside the lock.** Guidance, attachment reads and base64 pictures for prompt and steer are gathered by a separate fiber before the transition lock (`ProtocolPreparing`); the ready preparation wakes the driver, and only the transition and the record stay under the lock. Otherwise stdout reads and receipts would queue behind picture assembly. After Esc, a new prompt waits for the agent's answer to the cancel, and words meanwhile wait with reason `stopping`.
- **Cancel.** A record not yet handed is taken back from the inbox (`takeBack`). A handed one is withdrawn via `_tagma/withdraw`, and the `unqueue` reply is the outcome of that withdraw from the live reply memory (`Answers`): removed, not removed (the agent already took the word), or a `withdraw-failed` refusal if the withdraw failed. A refusal — because the word still stands unread, and `not-queued` would be untrue.
- **Turn end.** A new turn opens on the kernel follow-up wake, and no earlier than when all handed words of the ended turn received their fate (`WordsInFlight`): their reply decides whether the words return to the queue. This wait also holds Enter arriving in between: the word is kept in the queue and opens a turn together with the returned ones. The driver live-turn index changes after commit before the kernel-end publication and word fates: the first wake opens the next turn without waiting for settlement stages. A launched prompt is held by the row index, not simultaneously by the launch queue. Words that opened a turn stopped before the agent saw the prompt open no new turn: Esc means "don't do this", as on native. The kernel closes them together with the turn, even when the driver has not yet received the launch: without withdraw, reader words stay spoken in history and displace the previous rewind; withdraw moves them to the draft with the `withdrawn` end, and these words themselves do not hinder restore. An already-recorded task of another agent does not mean that reader inputs of the same opening are accepted or recorded. In a mixed opening, withdraw returns only reader words: the task stays spoken in history, and if it stands after rewind, restore still refuses. With such a withdraw, the opening reader message is not recorded even temporarily: attribution irreversibly displaces rewind when the message is added, and a later cut does not undo it. This is one kernel closing decision, not a driver opener with a later withdraw. No delivered words of this opening stay in the inbox. The driver holds the turn (`holds`) while the turn is open or until a reply arrives to a handed steer or a step compaction request, including after the turn end. A handed compaction request enters the same in-flight element accounting with the `handed` report after claim commit: otherwise an early wake would open an empty compaction run before the late confirmation of the already-started compression. An ended turn held by no transport — e.g. a turn the reaper stopped after a server crash — will no longer give its words fates: the follow-up itself removes them from accounting and opens the next turn, otherwise a word given to a stopping turn would wait forever. After an `aborted` turn end (Esc), unread steers and follow-ups open one turn in press order — ahead of compaction and regardless of its barrier, just as on the native thread; a steer the agent confirms only after that end is among them, already spoken ([[34be8310]]). Letters and reports of other agents that opened the turn are recorded by the kernel as `signal` events at opening on any transport: a queue — under the turn they were addressed to, after a stop — under the new turn (otherwise records would appear in a stopped turn after its end), and a record that itself woke the thread — under the new turn. A reader steer opening a turn together with a follow-up enters one user message in press order: the shared `openingWords` choice is used by the kernel and the ACP projection. On ACP these words are recorded when the prompt is accepted together with attachments of all merged reader inputs; the acceptance fact carries the exact ids of those inputs, so a late steer's attachments do not land in the earlier opening. Empty text with an attachment also leaves an opening message. The shared `threadUserMessageEvent` keeps attachments in both the feed and the model history; prompt confirmation closes the inbox with no second `signal` event and no second history message. A turn of steers alone keeps `signal` events. Otherwise an ACP turn opened by a child report would show the report only after its own reply. Likewise at opening the kernel records the task with which another agent opened the turn (`send_task`, the birth task): the transport records as the user message only words no other agent spoke (`spokenByAgent`), otherwise the task would stand in the journal twice. Inbox confirmation is given by the turn end (`fate`), and `fate('read')` does not rewrite a signal already standing in the journal. The task is recorded with its inbox record id, so a turn restarted after returned words neither repeats nor carries it into itself. Agent words stand in the journal before the same turn's operator words. If only such records opened the turn, it has no user message, like a native turn of unread words alone. A fork cut by turn count treats such a task as the turn start, as does the model history.

The contract is held by `protocolWords.stest.ts` (operator doors, the `ThreadEvidence` snapshot, a scripted ACP agent instead of the Claude runner, both reply orders on Esc) and `protocolMethods.stest.ts` (steer fates, lost receipt, restart).
