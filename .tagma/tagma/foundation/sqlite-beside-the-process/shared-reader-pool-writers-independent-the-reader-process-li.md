title: "Shared reader pool: writers independent, the reader-process limit belongs to runtime"
ord: 2
---
One `ProcessPool` serves the machine store and all its served mirrors. Each writer keeps its own child: a mirror's synchronous heavy write must not delay the machine journal. Reader processes shared; each holds independent SQLite connections of several stores, and one lane given to only one scoped read. The children limit is writers(stores) + K readers, not a constant across all processes.

`K = max(2, min(availableParallelism() − 1, floor(totalmem() / 32 / 64 MiB)))`. The CPU part leaves one thread to the runtime's other jobs; memory grants the reader engines a planned 1/32 RAM share with a 64 MiB allowance per child. That is no hard RSS cap: SQLite pages, mmap and results depend on data. On low-memory or single-CPU machines at least two readers keep point and bulk moving independently; the shared minimum does not multiply by stores. At 4 GiB RAM memory admits two readers, at 8 GiB four, beyond that CPUs also limit. 64 MiB is a planned reserve for the runtime and working SQLite pages, not a promise of each query's measured size.

A reader opens on first lane demand. A store's SQLite connection reused on the same lane, stays in its scope and closes on idle retirement or store close. The child ends after the last connection frees; not bound to the first store that opened it's lifetime. A pool created with `'keep'` leaves idle reader and checkpoint processes alive to its `close()`: so a test world opening stores again and again pays for them once, not on each open. Writers in that mode still end with their store. Bulk takes at most `K − max(1, floor(K/4))` lanes, point gets priority; the reserve shared across all stores. A store's `readers` limit caps its simultaneous reads. Waiting on the shared lane has the `sqlite.reader.shared.wait` span.

A child fall first closes all its channels, then publishes each connection's loss, and only after that wakes statements waiting with `SqlError`. Reader handles removed from local pools; the next demand opens a child anew. A query and transaction never replayed. Process isolation keeps surviving a reader's native crash without the server's death; worker threads give no such guarantee. The writer's loss still the caller's to decide. One shared writer rejected over head-of-line across stores; only a reduced per-store reader pool would also have left a constant process cost per idle store.

Isolated sample of 2026-10-03: darwin arm64, Bun 1.4.2, 12 CPU, 32 GiB RAM, other agents working alongside. `process.bench.ts` before the change: one child RSS p50 20.5 MiB; spawn + Open p50/p95 28.0/66.5 ms (8 launches); SELECT round-trip p50/p95 0.208/1.675 ms (800 statements). These numbers are the process cost in this sample, not policy parameters.

`readers.bench.ts`: three temporary WAL bases of one line each, 200 sequential rounds of three point reads, fingerprint `5d94d09e502c35c3`. Three alternating launches of the baseline from commit `1a92eb3540` and current code: reader children 3 → 1; RSS 63.05–63.44 → 22.83–22.88 MiB. Median of three run-level p50/p95 per round: 0.175/0.648 → 0.171/0.549 ms. Cold-open p50: 14.51 → 1.71 ms; the first child still requires spawn. `db.queue.ms` p95 = 0 in both groups, `db.exec.ms` p95 0.023–0.051 → 0.016–0.017 ms. The baseline temporarily reproduced from four package sources at the named commit and the same bench; temporary files deleted, current bench kept.

`isolation.bench.ts` with two stores and three children: while read A ran 8.31 s, ten commits of B had p50/p95 0.52/1.98 ms; with write A of 6.78 s — 1.23/3.19 ms. `sql.execute` split queue and exec, and both heavy statements had queue = 0. This verifies execution isolation, not tail latency of large production bases. After the operator's restart, the reader-children count on served-tree growth, RSS, `sqlite.reader.shared.wait` and queue/exec of identical fingerprints verified; the working server not loaded by these measurements.
