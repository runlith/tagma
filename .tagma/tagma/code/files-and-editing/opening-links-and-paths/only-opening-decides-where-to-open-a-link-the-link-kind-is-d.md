Only `Opening.open(link, how)` decides "beside or full place": a surface — feed, block body, Sources, Changes, commit card, terminal — builds a link with its kind's constructor (`fileLink`, `directoryLink`, `blockLink`, `commitLink`, `threadLink`, `siteLink`; a recognised path gets a file or directory from the `PathRoads` road by the `place_path` answer) and hands the gesture to the owner via `linkTarget` (element), `linkWithin` (delegated at the root, where outside render recreates elements — the block-body editor) or `opening.how(opening.modified(event))`. Otherwise each surface would decide on its own again, and the same link would open differently in the feed and in Knowledge.

- **The kind is declared by the thing's owner**, at its own `features/*/links.ts`: the beside place, the full place, tooltip words. A kind may have no beside place: a directory (`directory` kind at checkout) has nothing to stand in the right area, and the holder-tab worktree tree is banned by rule 19b8fd8a. Then click and ⌘-click open the full place, and the menu carries one opening item with the click's shortcut; a copy of the full place as "beside" would give two identical items in the menu. Each feature contributes its kinds to the `LinkKinds` point from its own layer, and `LinkRegistry` reads the point's current items, as `PaneRegistry` does for tabs: `opening` stands below all link showers and imports no kind. A kind with a right-area tab knows it (`tab`): "Open in …" in the area header builds a link from the tab id and opens its full place through `Opening`, a tab kind has no second full-place declaration. Menu items come through a separate `LinkMenuItems` point, each with a `kind` field, from any feature, so another feature's item ("Start agent on this" at a block) stands in someone else's kind menu with no central list.
- **The resolver is the surface's.** A kind resolves its services through a `Resolver` passed `linkTarget(opening, read, here)`: a link in a thread on the right or in another copy's graph finds `BlockTitles`, `ThreadChanges` and the rest in the scope of the place where it stands. Without `here` the window resolver acts (`Opening.attach` in `Window`).
- **The "beside" holder** is picked by the `Beside` port (composition fills from `SidePanes` and `ThreadSeats`): `from.thread` — the layout of the screen showing the source thread, otherwise the thread's own layout; with no thread — the holder's layout on screen. `standing` lets a kind find in the holder's layout the tab to move to the new thing instead of a second one (file with another row, active browser page); otherwise repeating the same thing moves to its tab by `sameContent`. Through the same port go file, block, commit and diff (the `change` kind puts it into the thread's Changes tab, a repeat moves to this tab instead of closing the diff; the same kind with no checkout carries the Scratch file, and the tab view shows it as the file itself; Changes rows open a diff and the Scratch file only by this link, list arrows — by its "beside" place; the `changes` kind is the thread's whole Changes tab: beside — this tab, full place — the Checkout Git tab). A thread beside is opened by the shell (`ShellOps`): a thread already shown main or as a tab unfolds in place. A site goes through the `Beside` port, and repeating the same link first looks for a standing page with this URL (`Beside.seek`) in the same layout where the port would put a new one: every `open_page` bears a new page on the server, and with no room for a tab `open_page` is not called — the link goes to the system browser. A kind the port refused (no right area on screen) opens the thing at the full place instead of staying silent.
- **Gesture into manner** is translated by the owner: the modifier is ⌘ on Apple, Ctrl otherwise; `links.click = section` swaps click and modifier; on the mobile shell "beside" becomes the full place because there is no right area. The terminal alone decides whose click it is: while the program tracks the mouse, a plain click is its, and the modifier plays the click's role.
- **A block page in a tab** (`BlockPage` with `aside`) hands the owner all its block anchors — breadcrumbs, outline rows, Links, view results, diff — with one `linkWithin` over `a[data-block]`; in Knowledge the same anchors stay the page's own moves, tied to the address. Its own copy's block gets its address at once, another's is searched via `BlockTitles`. A block row (`BlockRow`: outline, view results, Knowledge column tree and shelves) is one such anchor over the whole row, under its content. Its own row buttons (expand, actions menu, status, holder) stand above it and do not open the row. So the row is caught by the same `linkWithin` or the page move, not by its own click handler.
- **Document anchors are intercepted by one `Opening.listen`** (click and contextmenu on `document`). An anchor the kind knows as its own (`LinkKind.anchor`: at a site — http(s) of a foreign origin) follows the rule; there are no such interceptions on the mobile shell. A route anchor of its own origin — with no `target`, `download`, `rel=external` — stays a SvelteKit move on plain click. A kind may know such an anchor as its own (`LinkKind.route`: at a block — `a[data-block]`, in Knowledge these are breadcrumbs, Links, outline rows, view results, diff and the column tree). The copy — project, branch and diff — the kind takes from the anchor's own address. Searching the name anew, a block deleted in the branch would open beside from another branch where it still lives. In the column tree the tree element itself (`treeitem` with focus) serves as the row's link. So Enter, ⌘-Enter and the menu key act on it by the shared rule, not by the tree's own move. Then a click on it is its full place, the page move, and the modifier opens another place — beside, in the section's right area, as at `links.click = section`. Right click gives the kind's menu, where the first item is the full place with the click's shortcut, and "beside" stands with ⌘-click (`Opening.menu` with `click: 'full'`). Otherwise the menu would promise "beside" for a click that actually leaves the page. An outline row has its own actions menu, so its right click opens that menu at the pointer instead, with the same steps and kind items from `Opening.shape` and the same click: one row, one menu. Its listener stands on the row's anchor itself, because Svelte delegates `oncontextmenu` to the app root, which the event reaches only after the block tab's `linkWithin` root has opened the link menu. A foreign route anchor no kind knows goes inside the app on a modified click at any `links.click`, it has no menu. A server-attachment anchor (attachment, artifact) carries `target="_blank"` and stays with the page. Otherwise Chromium would open a window for a route, and the desktop host would hand the Tagma address to the system browser; `installExternalLinks` therefore refuses its own origin's window when the caller names the address a `page`, while still handing an attachment to the system browser. What the server serves as an attachment rather than a surface page is declared once — prefixes in `http/contract/wire/served.ts`; desktop (`surfacePage`), the service worker, the remote-door gates and artifact links read them, so a new attachment prefix will not turn in the window into a "page" that is not one.
- **There is no hover tooltip at a link.** It popped over every row and every word-link, repeated the name the row already shows, and retold click and ⌘-click at every hover; the operator rejected it as noise. The right-click menu teaches shortcuts: its opening items carry them. The exception is the terminal: a link there is only visible on hover, and while the program tracks the mouse the click belongs to it, so the strip at the panel corner (`LinkTip`, over `Opening.tip`) names the thing and the shortcut. `linkTarget` and `linkWithin` alike open on click and Enter (with the modifier — the full place) and give the menu on right click and the keyboard's contextual-menu system gesture (Menu key, Shift+F10, VO+Shift+M). At a keyboard `contextmenu` the target is the focused element, in the editor its root, not a link. So for a pointerless gesture whose target is the focused element itself, the owner (`pointed` in `linkTarget.ts`, read by `Opening.listen` for anchors too) takes the element at the selection start, i.e. under the caret. A gesture without a pointer is a `contextmenu` with an empty `pointerType`; at a browser sending it as a plain `MouseEvent` — with no right button and no Ctrl (Ctrl-click on Mac is a pointer). A mouse right click aims at the node under the pointer even over a focused root, so it does not open the link menu under the caret. In editable text this is the only path to a link from the keyboard: Enter enters text there, and Mod-Enter is taken by the line break (`HardBreak` from `StarterKit`). A dropdown-menu item carrying a link also gets `linkSelect` by its choice: space and releasing a stretched menu pointer choose the menu itself, and without it the item would close the menu without opening anything. `linkSelect` lets the click through, `linkTarget` opens it, so one link does not open twice. Having opened a link — by gesture or a place from the menu, but not by copying — the owner sends a bubbling `linkopened` event from its element, and popup windows with links ("This thread", Attention, Errors, Load) close on it (`onLinkOpened`), not on their own click handler: so the window closes after a Cmd-click and a menu item just as after a click, and no earlier than the item is chosen.
