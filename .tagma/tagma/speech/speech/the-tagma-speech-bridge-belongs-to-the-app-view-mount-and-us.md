An app view reaches `tagma/speech` with an `action`: `status`, `recognize`, `listening`, `transcript`, `finish`, `unsubscribe`, `speak`, `silence`. Subscriptions return a `subscription`; `tagma/notifications/speech` carries that address and the speech feed's `event`, `event: null` means the end, `failure` a refusal with a reason. An admission failure answers with an open refusal; after opening, a refusal travels as a failure notification. A data notification, including the end of a terminal recognize or transcript, can arrive before the open answer; a successful end keeps the answer carrying the address of an already finished subscription. A listening subscription stays open after recording ends and delivers a `listing` event with the new `Listening` on every change. A transcript subscription also carries `mute` and `mark` rows. `recognize` picks the microphone from `speech_status`: on the server machine the server captures, on a tethered device the window's existing `Sender` does. `finish` is called after the `state: live` event, rereads the tail and keeps the subscription until the end; `unsubscribe` and teardown cut it at once. A subscription and its capture also drop on connection loss, pause, or resume after background, and do not reopen automatically. A listening's project comes from the mount, the host creates the recognition address; another view does not finish it from a guessed address. `speak` receives the speech owner's `speech.synthesize` stream: first the selected voice and format, then PCM16 little-endian; the window's `Playback` plays the audio through Web Audio on the view's device. The `spoken` answer arrives after playback ends; stopped voicing answers with a refusal. `silence`, a new phrase and teardown remove only that mount's voicing. The server's `speak_text` and `stop_speaking` keep their own consumers and take no part in view voicing. Synthesis does not open the server's audio device. The window holds about two seconds of scheduled audio and then waits for playback before accepting the next frame. For a paced stream `LiveConnection.hold` opens a separate scoped channel: waiting for audio does not delay dispatch of the other feeds and the shared connection's heartbeat; removing the subscription closes the channel. The synthesis owner delivers frames as separate packets so RPC does not pack many seconds of audio into one Chunk. Another party's MCP App does not receive this method.
