The confirmed prefix of the old activity chain, on a one-time move to the base, recreates the file and bodies: the old bounded window is lost whole. An unrecognized ledger is kept and gets degraded service. On a successful open, the store closes the previous process's records with outcome `orphaned` and the time of the last note: the old process can no longer close them, and the last note is the closest estimate of the break moment. A failed startup keeps the file and gives degraded service: the record counts dropped facts, health names the cause and the resource, reading returns a typed error. A missing resource, permission failure, or implementation defect does not prove history is damaged, so automatic startup neither postpones the database nor passes an empty record off as restored.

The writer flushes a batch in one transaction; the batch's thread projects come from one query, and a missing mapping differs from a known `null`. Metadata is admitted ahead of canonical/folded bounded serialization: a fact's budget is a third of a page envelope, so actor/trigger/attrs and result can be read together. An overrun rejects the fact whole and counts as `metadata-ceiling` loss; the volume the serialization did not finish reading is unknown. A conversion portion is bounded by document bytes and yields to runtime between portions. Reads live under the store's shared ceiling, agreed with the number of held reader questions; a new read evicts the oldest one with its rows and membership. Continuing an evicted read needs a new question. Dropped facts, bodies, unattributed threads, and a failed write count as loss. After a failure the lane itself waits for the retry deadline and carries losses into the next batch, even if producers went quiet; lost facts are not reproduced. Health is available without the database and names pending, drops, losses, cause, and retry deadline. `ActivityLog.settled` confirms the record of accepted facts and bodies, or their losses, waiting on the writer to its retry deadline with no deadline of its own; it is interruptible and does not substitute confirmation with expiry. Synchronous log confirms the record at once. A read tries once to flush the queue; in-memory losses on failure enter coverage at once. Loss has a global scope: without trustworthy attribution it conservatively touches any question in its interval, not only the surviving rows of the selected threads. The interval is set by the query or the retention, regardless of rows present. Lost facts clear complete; lost bodies show by their state. Store shutdown first closes admission and waits for the flush and body writes until the settlement deadline. The settlement fiber belongs to the waiting call; on deadline the store owner frees body waiters and, through `Connections.retire`, removes its own SQL connections before the fibers waiting on them join. The SQL owner first wakes waiters, then removes its own writer process; the process exit has its own bounded deadline. `settled` keeps the count of the unfinished at deadline — queue, batch, and bodies in flight — and answers with it after close, without starting a new flush.

Bodies are separate files in hourly buckets. Admitting a body first reserves a confirmation slot in the writer queue: ordinary facts cannot take it before the worker answers. Without a slot the body is rejected before file write and chain head change; a body's loss and its bytes count independently of the fact-queue fill. The main flow checks the already-encoded bytes' size against the body ceiling — a share of the one-hour-storage budget — and hands the bytes to the worker. Text and JSON lie as they are, other types base64 with their type. A body row stores the content type and the size of the whole readable representation next to the stored one: the delta weighs little, and the reader gets the size to read the body whole by. The worker counts the size at write; expansion does not correct it at read. A request body is the bytes that went to the provider, and only a body marked `series` forms a chain. The worker stores it as a delta from the same thread's previous request in the same bucket: by top-level keys — changed values and array tails. Every few dozen links the request is written whole, so reading a body walks a bounded chain, not the thread's whole history. A lost link makes the body unreadable, but the activity row stays.

Retention first runs at the moment the store owner names (`upkeepAfter`; the server passes the local door opening, so that a transition attempt does not delete buckets ahead of the door), then on a timer, and costs in proportion to what leaves: bucket bytes are held by the `bucket` table, kept by triggers on `body`; the worker deletes buckets and at once forgets the chains that stood in them, so the thread's next request is written whole, not as a delta to a deleted file. First go buckets older than `activity.keepHours`, then the oldest beyond `activity.bodiesMaxBytes` with a `ceiling` loss; rows, notes, and bodies past their time are deleted by their own age, except those belonging to still-open activities.
