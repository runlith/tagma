Two Opus 5 runs at xhigh converge: five owners each, a residual of 206 and 200 lines, and the same three findings neither run was told to look for.

- **The cut.** A run ledger (committed steps, five deferred faces, the closed latch), a model attempt, a tool batch, a streaming-title owner, and a once-per-step injection latch. Between them one direction: the residual composes, and nothing imports it back.
- **The port law applied, not cited.** The title owner answers `read(event)` with the display to publish, and the second run's fold answers the events the reader may see; the residual pushes them. Neither owner holds the feed, so no channel travelled downward to be shared.
- **Dead weight found.** `agentToolSources` projects the catalog and is called by the runner, never by the loop; both runs moved it to `ToolCatalog`. No other contestant noticed it.
- **A collision caught.** Exporting the private `AgentToolOutcome` would put a second type of that name beside `AgentToolDoor`'s own. Run one renamed it `SettledToolCall`, run two `ToolCallOutcome`. Four other contestants exported the collision.
- **Cost.** 16 and 22 minutes, 80k and 108k written tokens — the most expensive field entry by a factor of three over Terra and thirteen over Astra.
- **Variance.** Two independent runs produced near-identical decompositions, which is what makes a single Opus run trustworthy evidence about Opus.
