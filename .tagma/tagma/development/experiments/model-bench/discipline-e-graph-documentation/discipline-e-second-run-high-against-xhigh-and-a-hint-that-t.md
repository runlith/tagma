The second run repeats the same region and the same brief with two changes. Opus and Sonnet run at `high` instead of `xhigh`, to price the rung; DeepSeek 4.1 Flash runs at `max`; and every contestant except Opus received one added section, "The comments in this code lie" — comments are never evidence or a source, every word of totality in one is verified against the code in each place it covers, and a claim whose only support is a comment is a defect, with each place the code contradicts a comment named in NOTES.md. DeepSeek, Muse and SWE-2 had the section in their brief from the start; Sonnet received it as a message about a minute into its run, after it had begun reading, so its result is a weaker reading of the hint. The question the run answers: does the hint make a cheap model read the `containing` line the first run's field copied from its comment, and what does a lower rung cost the two Anthropic models.

**Second-run order**, by the same rule as the first (a wrong sentence costs more than a missing one, then what was found): DeepSeek 4.1 Flash max, Sonnet 5 high, SWE-2 high, Muse Spark 1.3 max, Opus 5 high. Muse found the most comment-vs-code places of anyone and ranks fourth only for the one overbroad title; a reader who weighs findings over precision puts it second.

**Width is not a score.** Claim counts entered neither ranking as a merit; the rule was wrong sentences first, then what was found. Measured over titles and bodies, every draft of both runs lies between 1,450 and 2,520 words — nobody padded — and the words per claim run from 49 (Muse max, second run, the most compact writer) to 89 (Opus xhigh, the longest bodies). DeepSeek max's 37 claims hold about four a merger would drop as noise (a claim about a test, a one-line mechanism, two comment retellings), so its width is ~33 substantive invariants for 2,524 words; Opus xhigh's 24 are all substantive at 2,147 words, so the two are within a tenth of each other in invariants per word, and the ranking between them is decided by the wrong sentences (none either) and the findings, not by the count. A draft of 60 claims with 25 noise would rank below a draft of 20 with none: every claim is delivered over the lines it binds to whoever opens the file, so a noise claim is paid for by every later reader, and the wave briefs will say so — a claim the merger would drop counts against the draft, lighter than a false one.

**What the run showed.**
- The hint works on the cheap models, and works differently on each: DeepSeek turned it into the exact finding and one more, with narrow anchors and no wrong sentence; Muse turned it into three real findings and seven arguments with loose prose; SWE-2 found a true neighbour of the trap and walked past the trap itself. None of the three wrote the comment as a claim, which four of them did in the first run.
- One rung down on Opus cost the finding: Opus xhigh read `containing` correctly without a hint, Opus high copied its comment. Sonnet high without the hint at start avoided the trap but did not write it; with the hint it corrected a different comment (`placeOf`), fixed its own first-run anchor format, and cost more than its xhigh run because the late hint made it re-read.
- Read against price, the second run's first place cost $0.11 and its last place $2.38. The caveats stand: the hint was given to the cheap models and not to Opus, so this is not Opus xhigh against DeepSeek max on equal terms; DeepSeek max ran once, and the first run's two DeepSeek xhigh runs differed by a false body; and a hint against comments is a brief the builder was handed, not a property the model showed on its own — the next zone's trap will have another shape.
- Two of the three cheap models with the hint state the trap as a claim of their own, which is the form the catalog asks for: the container qualified, the exception referenced. That is the shape a builder's output should take, and it is now the acceptance criterion for the wave briefs: "where the code contradicts a comment, the contradiction is a claim, named in NOTES".
