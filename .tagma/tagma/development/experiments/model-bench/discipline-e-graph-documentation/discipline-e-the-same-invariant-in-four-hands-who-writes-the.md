Width and price do not say who writes well. This reading takes the best run of each of the four candidates — Opus 5 xhigh and Sonnet 5 xhigh from the first run, DeepSeek 4.1 Flash max and Muse Spark 1.3 max from the second — and sets the same invariant beside itself in four hands, judged by what the catalog asks of a claim: a title that reads alone as a search result with every condition it needs, a body that is only the reason, the boundary or the cost, and nothing the code already says. Words per claim over titles and bodies: Muse 49, DeepSeek 68, Sonnet 75, Opus 89.

**Coverage asked before the world** (`ProjectStores.at`). Opus: title 18 words, body 55 — "A refusal is louder than an absence … Asked first, both answer identically whatever the state." Sonnet: title 30 words carrying the consequence itself ("so a path outside a project's coverage and a path that does not exist on the machine answer identically"), body 38. DeepSeek: title 23, body 45, the same reason in the same order. Muse: title 15, body 33, the reason intact and nothing lost. All four paraphrase the comment above the code; here Muse wins outright — the shortest text with the whole reason.

**The recovery acts ask no coverage** (`WorktreeActs`). Opus: "Reading, resolving, taking or releasing an already prepared creation asks no coverage: the project on its record is the whole gate", and the body gives the consequence nobody else states — a creation whose store has since left coverage can still be read, taken or abandoned. Sonnet: the seven verbs named in the title, three and four, and a body with the reason for the three that re-ask. DeepSeek: the body enumerates what refuses the ungated acts instead — another project, another revision, never reserved — which is the mechanism, not the reason. Muse: folds the gate and the "not registered" word into one claim, two subjects in one node. Opus wins: the boundary AND what it permits.

**A repository nested inside a covered checkout.** Opus: "Coverage authorizes the reading of a place and git decides which store that place belongs to", body 60 words with the vendored-clone example and why git is asked last. Sonnet: "resolved as its own store, never as the checkout surrounding it", body 40. DeepSeek: put the fact in the area body and wrote the sharper neighbour as its claim (inside a covered checkout the registry is asked and its refusal travels). Muse: "resolves to absence" — 24-word body, the shortest, and the title overbroad, because a covered nested store is answered as itself; Luna, otherwise last, was the one who wrote that condition. Here brevity cost the condition.

**Coverage reasons leave prefixed** (`worktreeRefusal`). Opus: title 14, body 60 naming both reasons. DeepSeek: title 23, body 32 — names `foreign-namespace` and why it collides. Muse: title 16, body 11 — "Two facts under one code is a caller that cannot branch" — and drops WHICH reason collides, the one fact a reader arriving from search needs. Sonnet split it across two claims. DeepSeek wins: the specific fact and the reason in 55 words.

**What this says about each hand.**
- **Sonnet** writes the title the tag describes: long, in the product's words, standing alone with its condition and its consequence, so a reader from search needs no body — and its bodies are tight. Over ten thousand nodes that is the most valuable habit, because the title is what every listing, every finding and every search prints. Its second run leaned on identifiers in titles (`covers`, `Context.Service`); the brief will say product words.
- **Opus** writes the best reasons: its bodies carry the boundary and what would go wrong more completely than anyone's, at the cost of the longest bodies — a fifth could go without loss. Titles short and clean.
- **DeepSeek** is precise and balanced, and slightly mechanical: where the others say why, it sometimes says which check refuses. About one claim in ten is noise a merger drops. The brief will say "the reason, not the check".
- **Muse** is the most compact writer by a wide margin and, when the reason survives, the best text of the four; but compression drops the condition or the specific fact roughly once in every ten claims — an overbroad title in each of its runs, a body that names no reason. The brief will say "the specific fact stays: which reason, which condition".

Text quality, for the graph at scale: Sonnet, then Opus, then DeepSeek, then Muse — with Muse the one whose ceiling is highest if one instruction holds.
